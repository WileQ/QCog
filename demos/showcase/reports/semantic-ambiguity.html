Semantic ambiguity: context fixes a word's sense

Semantic ambiguity: context fixes a word's sense

module Bexact3 findings

Context shifts the dominant sense by -0.653, and a resolved sense is immune to later context.

What this model asks

How does the surrounding sentence select one sense of an ambiguous word, and why does asking about the sense first lock the interpretation in?

How it works

Senses as basis states of a three-level system, context as a unitary transformation acting before the sense question is asked; the order matters because the sense question is a measurement.

Classical baseline. Context as prior re-weighting: the sense distribution is updated by the context, and the same posterior is available before or after any other judgement.

References

  • Kintsch (2001), Predication, Cognitive Science 25, 173-202.
  • Blutner, Pothos & Bruza (2013), A quantum probability perspective on borderline vagueness, Topics in Cognitive Science 5, 711-736.

What this run shows

  • Without context the dominant sense 'sense 1' is chosen with probability 0.700; the sentence context moves it to 0.047 (-0.653), and the opposite context to 0.678.
  • Once the sense has been resolved, the same question asked again reproduces it with probability 1.000 (penalty 0.000): the interpretation is fixed and a later context cannot move it.
  • A prior-updating account predicts that the context can be applied at any time with the same effect, so this ordering asymmetry is the model's testable content.

Parameters: sense_1_weight = 0.7, context_strength = 0.9, context_scope = narrow. Backend: exact (exact). Runtime 0.0035 s.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • Three senses and one rotation angle are a schematic; real lexical disambiguation needs a corpus.
