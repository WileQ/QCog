What this model asks

When someone reports 'fifty-fifty', is the attitude genuinely unresolved, or settled but unknown? Response probabilities alone cannot tell the difference.

How it works

Coherent superposition versus statistical mixture: coherence is invisible to the question whose answer distribution it defined, and visible to any question incompatible with it.

Classical baseline. A classical model identifies a 50/50 belief with the distribution (1/2, 1/2) and therefore predicts that no other question can distinguish the two situations.

References

  • Busemeyer & Bruza (2012), Quantum Models of Cognition and Decision, ch. 1-2.
  • Pothos & Busemeyer (2013), Can quantum probability provide a new direction for cognitive modeling? BBS 36, 255-274.

What this run shows

  • Both the coherent belief and the classical ignorance state answer Q1 with p(0) = 0.500: the marginal cannot tell them apart.
  • They are separated by Q2: p(Q2=yes) is 1.000 for the coherent state, 0.925 after decoherence and 0.500 for the mixture (distinguishability index 0.500).
  • The decoherence sweep turns the coherent state continuously into the ignorance state: the difference between the two is coherence, and nothing else.
  • Asking Q1 first and Q2 second does not separate them, because the first sharp question erases the coherence; incompatibility has to be probed by asking the *other* question, not the same one again.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • Coherence is a property of the model state; nothing here claims coherence has been measured in a person.
  • With a single qubit, asking one sharp question erases exactly the coherence that a different sharp question would have revealed - which is why the model compares two separate questions rather than one question asked twice.
