module Dexact5 findings

Majority accuracy 0.787 versus Condorcet 0.874 for the same individual accuracy 0.70 (correlation 0.50).

What this model asks

How much better is a majority judgement than an individual judgement, and what does correlation between the judges do to that advantage?

How it works

A GHZ-style correlated state mixed with a product state; a single parameter moves the group from independent to perfectly correlated.

Classical baseline. Condorcet's jury theorem: independent voters with accuracy p give a majority accuracy that rises towards 1 with the group size.

References

  • Condorcet (1785), Essai sur l'application de l'analyse a la probabilite des decisions rendues a la pluralite des voix.
  • Ladha (1992), The Condorcet jury theorem, dependent voters and correlated votes, Public Choice 74, 331-342.

What this run shows

  • Majority accuracy is 0.7870 against an individual accuracy of 0.7000 (advantage +0.087).
  • With fully independent judges the same group would reach 0.8740, the Condorcet prediction: correlation between the judges is what is missing.
  • The correlation sweep falls monotonically from the Condorcet value to the individual accuracy, so the wisdom of crowds can be read off as a distance between two numbers.
  • The distribution of the number of votes in favour has mean 4.90 and variance 5.88, against the independent binomial variance 1.47: correlation is visible as over-dispersion.
  • With independent judges (correlation 0) the model reproduces Condorcet's 0.8740 exactly, which is the classical limit of this mechanism; a tie counts as no majority for both.

Parameters: num_agents = 7, individual_accuracy = 0.7, correlation = 0.5. Backend: exact (exact). Runtime 0.0000 s.

Circuit view

The drawn circuits appear above with the other figures:

  • GHZ preparation of the correlated component
  • the state 'state' is mixed (a density matrix), so it cannot be prepared as a circuit; the exact backend evaluates it directly
  • the correlated component is a GHZ state - the drawing shows how it is prepared - and the model then mixes it with a product state, which is a density matrix and is evaluated exactly

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • Correlation is modelled as a GHZ-style state; real groups correlate for many reasons, and the model does not distinguish between them.
  • A majority means strictly more than half, so an evenly split even-sized group has no majority; the classical baseline uses the same convention.
