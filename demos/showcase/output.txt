Superposition of beliefs versus classical uncertainty
module A - Individual cognition and decision making   |   slug: superposition-beliefs
============================================================================================
QUESTION  : When someone reports 'fifty-fifty', is the attitude genuinely unresolved, or settled but unknown? Response probabilities alone cannot tell the difference.
MECHANISM : Coherent superposition versus statistical mixture: coherence is invisible to the question whose answer distribution it defined, and visible to any question incompatible with it.
CLASSICAL : A classical model identifies a 50/50 belief with the distribution (1/2, 1/2) and therefore predicts that no other question can distinguish the two situations.

PARAMETERS (every one adjustable from Python: model.run(name=value))
    belief_angle             = 1.5707963267948966     float  [0.0, 3.141592653589793]
    probe_angle              = 1.5707963267948966     float  [0.0, 3.141592653589793]
    decoherence              = 0.15                   float  [0.0, 1.0]
    (executed exactly on the exact backend)

SUMMARY: Same marginal, different states: p(Q2=yes) = 1.00 (coherent) versus 0.50 (classical ignorance).

FINDINGS
  * Both the coherent belief and the classical ignorance state answer Q1 with p(0) = 0.500: the marginal cannot tell them apart.
  * They are separated by Q2: p(Q2=yes) is 1.000 for the coherent state, 0.925 after decoherence and 0.500 for the mixture (distinguishability index 0.500).
  * The decoherence sweep turns the coherent state continuously into the ignorance state: the difference between the two is coherence, and nothing else.
  * Asking Q1 first and Q2 second does not separate them, because the first sharp question erases the coherence; incompatibility has to be probed by asking the *other* question, not the same one again.

AGAINST THE CLASSICAL BASELINE: classical ignorance model
    A classical model that knows only the 50/50 answer distribution must predict the same distribution for every other question as well.
    quantity                                           quantum  classical
    distinguishability of the two states                 1.000      0.500
    -> The classical reference values are shown next to the model's; the difference between the two bars is the model's content, and where they agree the classical account is adequate.

QUANTUM CIRCUIT
    first then probe: 1 qubit, 2 classical bits, depth 6, 6 operations
    probe then first: 1 qubit, 2 classical bits, depth 6, 6 operations

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM
  - This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  - A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  - Coherence is a property of the model state; nothing here claims coherence has been measured in a person.
  - With a single qubit, asking one sharp question erases exactly the coherence that a different sharp question would have revealed - which is why the model compares two separate questions rather than one question asked twice.

Semantic ambiguity: context fixes a word's sense
module B - Logic, concepts and memory   |   slug: semantic-ambiguity
============================================================================================
QUESTION  : How does the surrounding sentence select one sense of an ambiguous word, and why does asking about the sense first lock the interpretation in?
MECHANISM : Senses as basis states of a three-level system, context as a unitary transformation acting before the sense question is asked; the order matters because the sense question is a measurement.
CLASSICAL : Context as prior re-weighting: the sense distribution is updated by the context, and the same posterior is available before or after any other judgement.

PARAMETERS (every one adjustable from Python: model.run(name=value))
    sense_1_weight           = 0.7                    float  [0.05, 0.95]
    context_strength         = 0.9                    float  [0.0, 1.5707963267948966]
    context_scope            = narrow                 choice    one of ['narrow', 'broad']
    (executed exactly on the exact backend)

SUMMARY: Context shifts the dominant sense by -0.653, and a resolved sense is immune to later context.

FINDINGS
  * Without context the dominant sense 'sense 1' is chosen with probability 0.700; the sentence context moves it to 0.047 (-0.653), and the opposite context to 0.678.
  * Once the sense has been resolved, the same question asked again reproduces it with probability 1.000 (penalty 0.000): the interpretation is fixed and a later context cannot move it.
  * A prior-updating account predicts that the context can be applied at any time with the same effect, so this ordering asymmetry is the model's testable content.

AGAINST THE CLASSICAL BASELINE: context as prior re-weighting
    A classical account updates the sense prior with the context; the posterior is available at any point, so asking about the sense first cannot make the context inert.
    quantity                                           quantum  classical
    p(dominant sense) with no context                    0.700      0.700
    change in p(dominant sense) caused by context       -0.653      0.000
    -> The classical reference values are shown next to the model's; the difference between the two bars is the model's content, and where they agree the classical account is adequate.

QUANTUM CIRCUIT
    sense: 2 qubits, 2 classical bits, depth 3, 4 operations

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM
  - This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  - A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  - Three senses and one rotation angle are a schematic; real lexical disambiguation needs a corpus.

Auctions, competitive bidding and the order of value and bid
module C - Strategic decisions and game theory   |   slug: auctions
============================================================================================
QUESTION  : Why are bids correlated with the order in which a bidder thinks about the item's value and about the competition?
MECHANISM : A three-level valuation question and a dichotomous bidding question that do not commute; a unitary rotation represents the information about rivals.
CLASSICAL : Risk-neutral bidding: the bid is a deterministic function of the (estimated) value, so no presentation order can change it.

PARAMETERS (every one adjustable from Python: model.run(name=value))
    valuation_confidence     = 0.55                   float  [0.05, 0.95]
    rival_pressure           = 0.6                    float  [0.0, 1.5707963267948966]
    bid_aggressiveness       = 0.8                    float  [0.1, 2.0943951023931953]
    (executed exactly on the exact backend)

SUMMARY: Bid after a high valuation 0.848 versus 0.076 after a low one; order shift -0.034.

FINDINGS
  * After answering that the item is high-value, the bidder bids high with probability 0.848; after a low valuation, with probability 0.076. The bidding question is genuinely sensitive to the valuation answer.
  * Reversing the order changes the valuation marginal by -0.034, which the order-invariant classical baseline cannot produce.
  * Thinking about rival bidders shifts the high bid by +0.173 in this model: rival information acts as a rotation of the belief, not as a change of the value estimate.
  * The practical warning is the classical one - a bidder whose bid depends on the order of their own deliberation cannot know whether the bid or the valuation was primary.

AGAINST THE CLASSICAL BASELINE: risk-neutral bidding
    The bid is a function of the value estimate, so the same estimate produces the same bid whatever order the questions arrive in.
    quantity                                           quantum  classical
    order effect on the valuation marginal              -0.034      0.000
    shift caused by information about rivals             0.173      0.000
    -> Every row compares the model with the value a classical account is forced to give - exactly zero - so the bars are the model's own content.

QUANTUM CIRCUIT
    valuation then bid: 2 qubits, 4 classical bits, depth 6, 8 operations
    bid then valuation: 2 qubits, 4 classical bits, depth 6, 8 operations

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM
  - This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  - A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  - The valuation question is a three-outcome model of a continuous estimate; the third outcome stands for the high-value region.

============================================================================================
Collective judgement: the wisdom of crowds and correlation
module D - Groups, social networks and collective behaviour   |   slug: collective-judgment
============================================================================================
QUESTION  : How much better is a majority judgement than an individual judgement, and what does correlation between the judges do to that advantage?
MECHANISM : A GHZ-style correlated state mixed with a product state; a single parameter moves the group from independent to perfectly correlated.
CLASSICAL : Condorcet's jury theorem: independent voters with accuracy p give a majority accuracy that rises towards 1 with the group size.

PARAMETERS (every one adjustable from Python: model.run(name=value))
    num_agents               = 7                      int    [3, 9]
    individual_accuracy      = 0.7                    float  [0.5, 0.95]
    correlation              = 0.5                    float  [0.0, 1.0]
    (executed exactly on the exact backend)

SUMMARY: Majority accuracy 0.787 versus Condorcet 0.874 for the same individual accuracy 0.70 (correlation 0.50).

FINDINGS
  * Majority accuracy is 0.7870 against an individual accuracy of 0.7000 (advantage +0.087).
  * With fully independent judges the same group would reach 0.8740, the Condorcet prediction: correlation between the judges is what is missing.
  * The correlation sweep falls monotonically from the Condorcet value to the individual accuracy, so the wisdom of crowds can be read off as a distance between two numbers.
  * The distribution of the number of votes in favour has mean 4.90 and variance 5.88, against the independent binomial variance 1.47: correlation is visible as over-dispersion.
  * With independent judges (correlation 0) the model reproduces Condorcet's 0.8740 exactly, which is the classical limit of this mechanism; a tie counts as no majority for both.

AGAINST THE CLASSICAL BASELINE: Condorcet jury theorem
    Independent judges with accuracy p: the majority accuracy is the tail of the binomial distribution and rises with the group size.
    quantity                                           quantum  classical
    majority accuracy                                    0.787      0.874
    variance of the vote count                           5.880      1.470
    -> The classical reference values are shown next to the model's; the difference between the two bars is the model's content, and where they agree the classical account is adequate.

QUANTUM CIRCUIT
    GHZ preparation of the correlated component: 7 qubits, 0 classical bits, depth 7, 7 operations
    note: the state 'state' is mixed (a density matrix), so it cannot be prepared as a circuit; the exact backend evaluates it directly
    note: the correlated component is a GHZ state - the drawing shows how it is prepared - and the model then mixes it with a product state, which is a density matrix and is evaluated exactly

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM
  - This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  - A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  - Correlation is modelled as a GHZ-style state; real groups correlate for many reasons, and the model does not distinguish between them.
  - A majority means strictly more than half, so an evenly split even-sized group has no majority; the classical baseline uses the same convention.
