## What was written during the build window

Everything in this repository: the library (`src/qcog/**`), the interactive explorer (`app/`), the examples, the
tests, the figures, the reports and the documentation. The project was started from an empty directory; there
was no pre-existing code base, repository or template.

The package design, the mathematical content, the wording of every model's findings and caveats, the
accessibility approach and the evidence table were written for this project. Component-level choices are
documented in the code.

## Scientific literature

Q-Cog models phenomena reported in the literature and cites its sources in two places: each model's
`references` list (rendered by `qcog info`, in the explorer and in the report) and the docstrings of the
relevant modules. The sources used for the phenomena, models and tests include:

* Busemeyer & Bruza (2012), *Quantum Models of Cognition and Decision* - the general framework.
* Pothos & Busemeyer (2013), *Can quantum probability provide a new direction for cognitive modeling?* (BBS).
* Wang & Busemeyer (2013) and Wang, Solloway, Shiffrin & Busemeyer (2014, PNAS) - question-order effects and the
  QQ equality.
* Moore (2002) - empirical question-order effects.
* Tversky & Kahneman (1983) - the Linda conjunction problem.
* Tversky & Shafir (1992) and Shafir & Tversky (1992) - the disjunction effect.
* Busemeyer, Pothos, Franco & Trueblood (2011) - interference accounts of probability judgement.
* Aerts & Gabora (2005), Hampton (1987) - concept combination and emergence.
* Eisert, Wilkert & Lewenstein (1999) - quantum games; DeGroot (1974), Hegselmann & Krause (2002), Condorcet
  (1785), Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer & Welch (1992) - the classical baselines for the group models.

No text, code or figure was copied from these sources. What was taken from them is: the questions, the
qualitative phenomena, the classical baselines, and the quantitative identities that the library then
re-derives and tests (for example the QQ equality, the conjunction bound, Condorcet's probability and the
classical prisoner's dilemma payoffs).

## Data

`src/qcog/data/benchmarks.json` contains **illustrative synthetic data generated by Q-Cog itself** with a fixed
seed. It is explicitly *not* the published values from the papers: no published dataset was copied into this
repository. Its purpose is to exercise the fitting and model-comparison workflow. Provenance and generating
parameters are recorded inside the file and in [`src/qcog/data/README.md`](src/qcog/data/README.md).

## Use of AI assistance

AI coding assistance was used during development, as the hackathon rules explicitly permit and encourage. The
division of responsibility is documented here for transparency:

* The mathematical constructions, the choice of which identities to verify, the classical baselines, the
  caveats and the scientific claims are grounded in the cited literature and are checked by the test suite and
  by `qcog evidence` - the executable checks are the authority, not the prose.
* Every model's claims were run and inspected before being documented; where a claim could not be verified it
  was removed or reframed as an explicit caveat (see the game-theory convention note in
  [`docs/limitations.md`](docs/limitations.md)).
* The self-check table exists precisely so that a reader does not have to take any of this on trust:
  `qcog evidence` reproduces or falsifies each claim in about a minute.
