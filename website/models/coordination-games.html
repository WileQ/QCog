Coordination games: conventions without communication

module C: Strategic decisions and game theoryintermediateslug: coordination-games

Coordination probability 1.000 (classical equilibrium 0.500) with shared context 1.57 rad.

What it asks

How do two agents who cannot talk still end up at the same convention?

How it works

Correlated strategy choice through entanglement: both players' moves are perfectly correlated in the appropriate strategic basis, with no signalling.

Classical baseline. The symmetric mixed-strategy equilibrium of the coordination game, which coordinates with probability 1/2.

What this run shows

  • Probability that both players choose the same convention: 1.000, against the classical mixed equilibrium's 0.5 (expected payoff 2.00 versus 1.00).
  • With no shared context the model reproduces the classical 0.5 exactly, which is the sanity check for the whole construction.
  • The shared-context sweep rises to perfect coordination: the players never communicate, they simply share a context.
  • Decoherence is the failure mode to watch: losing the shared context returns the game to the classical coin-flip, smoothly and without a threshold.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • The correlation is a feature of the modelled situation (a shared context), not a claim that people share quantum states.
