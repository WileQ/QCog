Quantum opinion dynamics on a network

module D: Groups, social networks and collective behaviouradvancedslug: quantum-opinion-dynamics

Spread 0.774 -> 0.146 over 8 rounds; agreement 0.00; mean local entropy 0.80 bits.

What it asks

How do opinions spread through a group when each encounter is an exchange rather than an average?

How it works

Partial-swap interaction on pairs of qubits: a coherent exchange that creates superposition, plus decoherence as forgetting.

Classical baseline. DeGroot averaging on the same graph: opinions converge to a weighted average and the final spread is the consensus value.

What this run shows

  • Opinions move from a spread of 0.774 to 0.146; agreement (|mean|) is 0.000 and the number of clusters is 1.
  • The classical DeGroot baseline on the same graph converges to a consensus value of -0.000 with a spread of 0.056.
  • The average local entropy rises to 0.795 bits: agents end up in superpositions of positions, which is what a coherent exchange does and an averaging rule cannot.
  • Decoherence is the classical limit: with enough forgetting the trajectory approaches the averaging baseline, because the correlations and superpositions are destroyed.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • The dense simulation limits the population to ten agents; larger networks need the classical baselines or a tensor-network implementation.
