Ambiguity, evidence accumulation and framing

module A: Individual cognition and decision makingadvancedslug: belief-revision

Framing changes the effect of identical evidence by +0.093.

What it asks

Does the way a belief is framed change what the same evidence does to it, and does evidence accumulate the way Bayes' rule says it should?

How it works

Unsharp (POVM) measurements for weak evidence, repeated state updates for accumulation, and a unitary framing transformation that does not commute with the evidence.

Classical baseline. Bayesian updating: the posterior depends on the likelihood ratio and the prior, not on how the question was framed.

What this run shows

  • The framing rotation changes the immediate response to the evidence by -0.418.
  • After the same evidence the follow-up judgement differs by +0.093 between the two framings, where a likelihood-based classical model predicts exactly 0.
  • Repeated weak evidence converges geometrically to the evidence eigenstate, but the path depends on the framing: the same number of observations can leave two people at different beliefs.
  • The convergence is not a learning curve: no new information arrives, the state is projected again and again.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • The evidence operator models 'how much this observation moves the judgement', not a statistical likelihood.
