Deliberation, plurality and Condorcet cycles

module D: Groups, social networks and collective behaviourintermediateslug: deliberation-voting

Winner option A -> option B after deliberation (previous winner share change -0.535); Condorcet cycle: False.

What it asks

Does discussing an issue before voting change which option wins, and can a group prefer A to B, B to C and C to A at the same time?

How it works

A three-level preference state, a unitary deliberation operator, and a three-outcome measurement for the vote; pairwise preferences are further questions on the same state.

Classical baseline. Classical preference aggregation: a fixed preference ordering per voter, so pairwise comparisons are transitive for each voter and the aggregate is only cyclic if the voters disagree (Condorcet).

What this run shows

  • Before deliberation the plurality winner is option A (0.550); after deliberation it is option B (0.835). Change in the previous winner's share: -0.535.
  • Pairwise comparisons: option A 0.978 vs option B 0.022; option B 0.971 vs option C 0.029; option A 0.910 vs option C 0.090.
  • The pairwise majority relation is transitive here, which is the classical benchmark: the model reports it explicitly so that a reader can see whether deliberation changed the *ranking* or only the shares.
  • The deliberation sweep shows how much discussion is needed to change the winner, and the coherence parameter moves the pairwise comparison between the top two options across the 50% line - so the same preference state can look decisive or indifferent depending on the phase.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • The three options are modelled as basis states of one voter's belief; a real electorate needs an aggregation model on top of this.
  • With this library's pairwise questions (coherent comparisons of two options) the majority relation comes out transitive; a Condorcet cycle needs context-dependent pairwise questions or aggregation across voters, and the model reports whether a cycle occurred rather than assuming one.
