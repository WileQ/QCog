Information cascades and the order of private and social information

module D: Groups, social networks and collective behaviouradvancedslug: information-cascades

6/6 adoptions; cascade onset at agent 2; classical posterior 0.976.

What it asks

Why does an agent who has private evidence follow the herd, and why does it matter whether the group or the evidence is consulted first?

How it works

Two incompatible questions per agent - the private signal and the social context - asked in a specified order, with the social context encoded as a rotation that grows with the observed majority.

Classical baseline. Rational Bayesian social learning: an agent uses Bayes' rule on the observed history and its private signal, which makes the decision independent of the order in which they are consulted.

What this run shows

  • 6 of 6 agents adopted; the first agent whose decision no longer depended on the private signal appears at position 2.
  • Under rational Bayesian learning the posterior after the same observed decisions would be 0.976 for the state the cascade favours.
  • With the signal consulted first, the decision is anchored on the private evidence and the social context only tilts it; with the group consulted first, the same evidence produces a different decision. A Bayesian agent has no such order dependence.
  • The social-pressure sweep shows the threshold behaviour practitioners care about: below a certain pressure the sequence tracks the signals, above it the group decides.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • The social context is modelled as a rotation of the belief, not as Bayesian evidence; the model is a description of order-sensitive decision making, not a claim that people are irrational.
