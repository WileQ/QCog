Evolution of cooperation under quantum strategies

module C: Strategic decisions and game theoryadvancedslug: evolution-of-cooperation

Final composition C/D/Q = 0.99/0.01/0.01 (classical baseline 0.01/0.98/0.01); cooperative share 0.99 at gamma = 1.57 rad.

What it asks

If strategies are copied according to success, does cooperation survive in a population, and does a quantum strategy dominate a classical one?

How it works

Coherent strategy space in repeated interactions: the payoff matrix used by the evolutionary dynamics is computed from the quantum game, so entanglement changes which strategy is fittest.

Classical baseline. The replicator dynamic on the classical prisoner's dilemma payoff matrix, where defection takes over from any interior starting point.

What this run shows

  • Starting from mostly defectors, the population ends at C = 0.987, D = 0.007, Q = 0.007; with the classical payoff matrix it ends at C = 0.007, D = 0.980, Q = 0.013.
  • With no entanglement defection takes over (0.007 of the population defects), which is exactly what the classical analysis predicts.
  • Cooperative play (C or Q) reaches 0.993 of the population here, and the sweep puts the transition at about gamma = 0.56 rad.
  • The surviving cooperative strategy is classical C, not Q: in this convention a defector can still exploit a Q player, so evolution selects the strategy that is not exploitable even though Q is the strategy that makes mutual cooperation the payoff-dominant outcome. The sweep shows which strategy wins as a function of the entanglement, rather than assuming one.
  • Mutation prevents fixation, so a cooperating population is never permanently safe.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • The evolutionary dynamics is classical; only the payoff matrix comes from the quantum game. This hybrid is deliberate and is the standard way to ask an evolutionary question about a quantum game.
