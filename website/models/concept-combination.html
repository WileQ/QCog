Concept combination: emergent and destroyed features

module B: Logic, concepts and memoryintermediateslug: concept-combination

'fins': parts 0.00/0.25 versus combination 0.84 (emergence +0.595).

What it asks

Why is a 'pet fish' neither a typical pet nor a typical fish, and why do combined concepts have features that neither part has?

How it works

A combination operation on the tensor product of two concepts; typicality is the projector of a feature question, and non-separability creates joint structure.

Classical baseline. Feature typicality of the combination as a weighted average of the typicalities of the parts (the standard extensional baseline).

What this run shows

  • Typicality of 'fins' for the parts: 0.000 and 0.250; for the combination 0.845 (their average is 0.125).
  • Emergence: the combination exceeds the more typical part by +0.595, which the weighted-average baseline forbids.
  • The combined state's entanglement is 0.1029 bits (concurrence 0.2303), so the joint structure is not a product of the parts.
  • Features that are atypical in both parts but typical in the whole - and typical features that the combination destroys - are both produced by the same unitary, and the sweep over the combination strength moves between them.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • Typicality ratings are modelled as probabilities of a feature question; the mapping from rating scales to probabilities is a modelling choice.
