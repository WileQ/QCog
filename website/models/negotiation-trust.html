Negotiation, trust and shared context

module C: Strategic decisions and game theoryintermediateslug: negotiation-trust

Agreement probability 0.963 with shared context 1.57 rad and suspicion 0.05.

What it asks

Why do two parties reach a mutually profitable agreement when each would be worse off if the other backed out?

How it works

Entanglement as shared context: correlated outcomes let both parties choose the risky, profitable strategy without communicating, and decoherence models suspicion or miscommunication.

Classical baseline. The mixed-strategy equilibrium of the stag-hunt game, in which agreement is reached less often than the payoff-dominant outcome.

What this run shows

  • Probability both parties commit: 0.963; the first party commits with probability 0.975.
  • Expected payoffs (3.93, 3.93) against the classical mixed equilibrium, which hedges with probability 0.25.
  • The shared-context sweep is monotone: with no shared context both parties hedge, and with maximal context the profitable commitment is reached without any communication.
  • Suspicion (decoherence) is the interesting failure mode: it removes the correlation the agreement relied on while leaving the payoffs untouched.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • 'Trust' here is exactly the correlation between the two parties' strategic states; the model does not claim that trust is entanglement.
