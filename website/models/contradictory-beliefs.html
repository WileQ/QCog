Contradictory beliefs and contextual measurements

module B: Logic, concepts and memoryadvancedslug: contradictory-beliefs

Self-report and behaviour differ by 0.337 from what a joint distribution allows; QQ difference +0.0000.

What it asks

How can someone sincerely report one attitude and behave differently, without either report being a lie?

How it works

Incompatible (non-commuting) questions whose contexts cannot be jointly realised; a contextuality certificate replaces the assumption of a single joint distribution.

Classical baseline. Any classical account requires a joint distribution over self-report and behaviour, so the marginal of the report cannot depend on the order in which the two were elicited.

What this run shows

  • Contextuality certificate: the largest law-of-total-probability violation is 0.337. Any classical joint distribution over the two answers forces this number to be exactly 0.
  • The two orders are a total-variation distance of 0.337 apart, which is the amount by which a single joint distribution would have to be wrong.
  • The question sharpness sweep is the interesting comparison: turning both questions uninformative removes the contradiction and restores the classical picture.
  • Asking the other question first moves p(self-report = yes) by -0.337 (TV distance 0.3374). Question incompatibility = 1.9088.
  • QQ equality holds: same-answer agreement is 0.8690 in one order and 0.8690 in the other (difference +0.0000, z = +0.00).
  • Law of total probability violated: p(self-report=yes) measured directly = 0.8374, but computed from the other order = 0.5000 (violation +0.3374)
  • Law of total probability violated: p(self-report=no) measured directly = 0.1626, but computed from the other order = 0.5000 (violation -0.3374)
  • The classical (law-of-total-probability) part of p(behaviour=yes) is 0.749 and the interference term is -0.249, i.e. interference supplies -49.8% of the probability. Interference can be negative, which is what makes the two question orders disagree.
  • A classical model answers both questions from a single joint distribution, so it predicts a marginal shift of exactly 0; the model predicts 0.3374. The (unfitted) classical baseline is therefore rejected by construction whenever this is nonzero - which is a statement about the model, not yet about people.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • A contextuality certificate for the model is not a psychological diagnosis, and the model has no opinion about which of the two questions is the 'real' attitude.
