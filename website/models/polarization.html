Polarisation and information bubbles

module D: Groups, social networks and collective behaviouradvancedslug: polarization

Community gap 1.20 -> 0.83; mean local entropy 0.13 bits.

What it asks

Why do groups with the same information drift apart, and what changes when the exchange between them is coherent rather than an average?

How it works

Partial-swap exchange restricted by network structure, with decoherence as the mechanism that converts coherent uncertainty into a stable position.

Classical baseline. Hegselmann-Krause bounded confidence on the same graph, which preserves clusters, and DeGroot averaging, which erases them.

What this run shows

  • The gap between the community means moves from 1.200 to 0.833; within-community agreement is +0.416 (community 1) and -0.416 (community 2).
  • The bounded-confidence baseline keeps a gap of 1.129, so the two models make different predictions about the same network - which is the point of reporting both.
  • The average local entropy is 0.134 bits: coherent exchange leaves agents in superpositions, so agreement between their answers can hide disagreement inside their states.
  • The cross-talk sweep is the levers-on-the-model version of the question: a single bridge changes the outcome by a computable amount, and decoherence decides whether the change sticks.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • The model says nothing about which side is right and treats the initial positions as given.
