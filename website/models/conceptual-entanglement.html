Conceptual entanglement: joint structure without interference

module B: Logic, concepts and memoryintermediateslug: conceptual-entanglement

Joint probability 0.500 versus independence 0.250 (concurrence 1.00).

What it asks

How can a combined concept have joint structure - features that always co-occur - when neither part determines it?

How it works

Nonseparable (entangled) states: correlations in the joint distribution that no product state reproduces, quantified by concurrence and the entropy of entanglement.

Classical baseline. Statistical independence of the two features, p(A and B) = p(A) p(B), which is the best a product state can do.

What this run shows

  • Each feature alone is present with probability 0.500 and 0.500; together they are present with probability 0.500, against the independence prediction 0.250 (excess +0.250).
  • Concurrence 1.000 and entropy of entanglement 1.000 bits measure exactly this joint structure, and both are maximal at a combination angle of pi/4.
  • The joint probability never exceeds the smaller marginal, unlike the conjunction fallacy: commuting feature questions are jointly answerable, so the effect is correlation rather than interference. The two mechanisms must not be conflated.
  • Decoherence shrinks the correlation smoothly to zero, turning the combined concept back into a product of its parts.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • Entanglement here is a property of the modelled concept combination; it is not a claim that concepts are physically entangled.
