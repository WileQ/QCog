Conjunction fallacy (the Linda problem)

module B: Logic, concepts and memoryintermediateslug: conjunction-fallacy

p(conjunction) = 0.615 exceeds the classical bound 0.346 by 0.269; independent attributes give -0.045.

What it asks

Why do people rate 'Linda is a bank teller and a feminist' as more probable than 'Linda is a bank teller', when no probability space allows it?

How it works

Destructive interference between the components of the belief state in the direct bank-teller question; the conjunction is evaluated after the feminist question has collapsed the state, so it keeps the higher conditional probability.

Classical baseline. The conjunction rule p(A and B) <= min(p(A), p(B)), which holds in every classical probability space and is also exact for commuting (independent-attribute) quantum questions.

What this run shows

  • Model: p(feminist) = 0.869, p(bank teller) = 0.346, p(bank teller and feminist) = 0.615.
  • The classical upper bound is 0.346, so the model exceeds it by +0.269: the conjunction is judged more probable than one of its conjuncts.
  • The whole effect comes from the phase of the belief state: it makes the two components interfere destructively in the direct bank-teller question, while the conjunction is asked of a collapsed state. The phase sweep crosses zero exactly where the fallacy disappears.
  • With independent attributes the joint probability would be p(feminist) x p(bank teller) = 0.301, which cannot exceed the bound (-0.045): the fallacy is a signature of incompatible questions, not of uncertainty as such.
  • Classical bound 0.346 versus the |teller> conditional 0.708 - the gap a judge can check by hand.

What this does not claim

  • This is a model of response probabilities, not a claim about what the brain does.
  • A quantum model only means the data are described by a quantum probability space; classical models may fit equally well, and the classical baseline is reported for that reason.
  • The model reproduces the fallacy with a phase; it does not claim that beliefs literally have phases.
  • The independent-attribute variant of the same model satisfies the classical bound exactly, so the fallacy is a property of incompatible questions, not of uncertainty as such.
